# English RPG — 300-Day A2+ to Strong B1/B2 Roadmap

## Goal

This plan is designed for **Asilbek**, a backend developer currently around **A2+**, with ideas sometimes reaching B1 but grammar accuracy still at A2 level.

**Daily time:** 60 minutes  
**Duration:** 300 study days  
**Realistic target:** strong B1, with a possible early B2 result if speaking practice is consistent  
**Main uses:** daily life, work, programming, meetings, interviews, relationships, travel, and independent learning

This plan develops all four skills, but speaking and writing receive extra attention because passive understanding alone does not create fluency.

---

## Non-Negotiable Daily System

Unless a day gives a different breakdown, use:

- **10 minutes — Review:** Review yesterday's corrections and vocabulary.
- **15 minutes — Learn:** Study the day's grammar, vocabulary, or pronunciation.
- **15 minutes — Input:** Listen to or read understandable English.
- **15 minutes — Output:** Speak aloud or write without translating first.
- **5 minutes — Error log:** Record 1–3 corrected mistakes.

Use a timer. Stop after 60 minutes. Consistency is more important than occasional long study sessions.

## Required Notebook Sections

Keep these sections in a paper notebook or one digital note:

1. **Error log:** wrong sentence, corrected sentence, reason.
2. **Useful chunks:** complete phrases such as “I’m interested in…”, not isolated words.
3. **Speaking log:** date, topic, recording length, biggest problem.
4. **Writing portfolio:** keep every weekly text so progress is visible.
5. **Vocabulary deck:** add no more than 8 useful cards per normal day.

## Core Resources

### Main books

- **Essential Grammar in Use with Answers — Raymond Murphy:** use first if *English Grammar in Use* feels difficult.
- **English Grammar in Use with Answers, 5th edition — Raymond Murphy:** the main grammar reference from approximately Day 61 onward.
- **English Vocabulary in Use: Pre-intermediate and Intermediate — Stuart Redman:** use by topic; do not memorize the whole book.
- **English Pronunciation in Use: Elementary or Intermediate:** choose the level that feels understandable.
- **Short Stories in English for Intermediate Learners — Olly Richards:** begin around Day 91.

Buy one grammar book, not all of them at once. The free resources below are enough to start.

### Free resources

- [British Council LearnEnglish](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/) — grammar, reading, listening, writing, and speaking practice by level.
- [VOA Learning English](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/) — slow, clear American English; use Level 2 and intermediate material.
- [BBC Learning English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/) — short lessons, pronunciation, vocabulary, and everyday English.
- [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/) — definitions, example sentences, grammar, and UK/US audio.
- [YouGlish](https://youglish.com/) — hear words and phrases in real video examples.
- [Cambridge Test Your English](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/test-your-english/) — informal level checks.
- [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) — optional spaced-repetition vocabulary tool.
- [TED](https://www.ted.com/talks) — later listening and presentation practice.

### Resource rules

- Use an English definition first; translate only when still confused.
- Save phrases with examples: `interested in programming`, not only `interested`.
- For videos: listen once without subtitles, once with English subtitles, then summarize.
- Do not use native-language subtitles after Day 30.
- AI may correct your work, but write or speak first. Ask it to explain only your three most important errors.

# Weekly and Monthly Measurement

## Every 7 study days

Record:

- Minutes completed out of 420.
- New useful phrases retained, not merely added.
- One speaking duration without stopping.
- Number of repeated errors.
- One thing to change next week.

## Every 30 study days

Score yourself from 1 to 5:

| Area | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluency | Frequent long stops | Can continue with some hesitation | Speaks smoothly for the task |
| Grammar | Errors often block meaning | Errors exist but meaning is clear | Mostly accurate at current level |
| Vocabulary | Mostly basic isolated words | Enough phrases for familiar topics | Varied, precise chunks |
| Pronunciation | Often difficult to understand | Generally understandable | Clear stress, endings, and rhythm |
| Listening | Understands isolated words | Understands main idea and details | Follows normal material for the level |
| Writing | Disconnected sentences | Organized paragraphs | Clear, cohesive, well-edited text |

## Minimum success criteria at Day 300

You should be able to:

- Speak for 10–15 minutes about familiar or professional topics.
- explain a backend system in clear, non-technical and technical language.
- participate in a basic English meeting and ask for clarification.
- write a clear 300–450 word text.
- understand the main ideas of normal B1 material.
- use past, present, future, perfect forms, modals, and conditionals with reasonable accuracy.
- notice and correct many of your own common mistakes.

# Rules for Missed Days

- Do not complete two full lessons in one day.
- If you miss one day, continue with that numbered day tomorrow.
- If you miss a week, spend one return day reviewing, then continue.
- Never restart from Day 1 because of a break.
- A 20-minute reduced session is better than zero: 5 minutes review, 10 minutes input, 5 minutes speaking.

# High-Value Correction List for Asilbek

Review these until they become automatic:

- `I am a backend developer`, not `I am backend developer`.
- `I work for a local company`, not `I work local company`.
- `I am interested in programming`, not `interested on programming`.
- `I am planning to get married`, not `planning to marriage`.
- `I sat all day`, not `I seat all day`.
- `I learned something related to my career`, not `learnt something related my career`.
- `The most difficult decision I made`, not `the best difficult decision which I make`.
- `I studied a subject that I wasn't interested in`, not `a path which I don't interest`.
- `If I had known about the meeting, I would have come earlier.`
- `I have lived here for five years` means the situation continues now.
- `I lived there for five years` describes a finished past period.

# Final Principle

Do not measure progress by how many lessons you consume. Measure it by what you can understand and produce without help. Every week must contain real speaking, real writing, correction, and repetition.
